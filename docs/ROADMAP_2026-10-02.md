# Rig roadmap: calibration v2, the HPC portal, learning and cloth

*2 October 2026. Written for the rig's owner, and for later Claude sessions on the rig. Research only: nothing in this document was run on the robot, and this file is the only change to the repo.*

**How to read it.**
- `[n]` points to the numbered sources at the end. Only sources that an independent check confirmed are used, or official docs and repos. Anything else says *(unconfirmed)*.
- Rig facts come from this repo's code, configs and notes, and from the uploaded session transcripts. Links to repo files are relative to `docs/`.
- Labels: **(measured)** someone measured it, with a source; **(computed)** arithmetic or FK on the URDF, done by this research; **(estimate)** a prior, meant to become a preregistered gate; **(unconfirmed)** not verified.
- Each suggestion ends with a closing block: the **First step**, the **Pass** line to preregister, and what it **Touches** (files and functions), **Measures now** and **Would measure**.
- §1.0 says what to do this week. §9 gives the order after that.

**Words used below.**
- **TCP** (tool centre point): a frame at the claw tip, the point the IK should steer.
- **X-corner**: the point where four checkerboard squares meet. A camera can locate it to a fraction of a pixel.
- **Bundle adjustment**: the single least-squares fit in §3.4 that solves zeros, backlash, stiffness, link errors and camera poses together.
- **RTC** (real-time chunking): blending each new chunk of actions into the one still running, so network delay does not stall the arm (§4.4).
- **DAgger**: retraining on the operator's corrections. HG-DAgger and RaC are versions where the operator takes over mid-run. **AWR** and **RECAP** learn from the robot's own good and bad attempts.
- **MIG**: one GPU split into smaller slices.

### 2.2 Safety rules that apply to everything below

From [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) and [`VR_TELEOP.md`](VR_TELEOP.md):
- Never use `Humanoid()` or the stock `write_configurations.py`: on this PC they map can0/can1 to the **legs**. The same holds for every stock BHL script that constructs `Humanoid()`, such as `calibrate_joints.py` (§3.8).
- Never rewrite a motor's `device_id` or flux offset. Every calibration result in this document lives in the host-side profile, never in motor firmware. Flux-offset sweeps (`tools/calibrate_arm_joint.py`) write motor flash, so they are outside this plan. Only you run them, by hand. A Claude session never proposes or runs one.
- Restart `run_teleop.py` only when it is STOPPED with the motors off and the arms hang still at zero: it takes zero at start-up. Once the corrected zero of §3.1 lands, the CLAUDE.md check "every `q` should be near 0" becomes "every `q` near the stored hanging pose `q_hang`".
- Any remote or learned policy goes through the robot's own safety layer: grip dead-man, joint ranges, the 1.5 rad/s and 0.35 rad clamps, the calibrated torque caps, and hold-then-retract when the policy link goes quiet (§4.5).

Two more rules the transcripts earned:
- **No blind motion.** Several sessions drove a joint into the torso because nobody could see the arm (§3.4). Any new automatic motion gets a self-collision and environment check, and a camera direction check before a long move. It is built on the calibration's `_move` and `_hold`, which freeze when the grip is released, and every pose list is dry-run in `--sim` first. The rig has no hardware e-stop: the stops are triple-click X in the headset ([bring-up notes](../arm_validation/TELEOP_AND_ARM_BRINGUP.md), item 11: "there was no physical e-stop in the Quest path") and Ctrl+C on the PC. Each new feature ships with a `--sim` test in the style of [`tools/test_teleop_tuning.py`](../tools/test_teleop_tuning.py), which drives `run_teleop` against `SimArmDriver` and a scratch copy of the profile.

### 3.1 What calibration measures today, and one test to run first

Today's power calibration (`PowerCalibration._script` in [`arm_calibration.py`](../scripts/teleop/arm_calibration.py)) runs: zero pose → reach → motors on (with a load check) → per joint, in the order pitch, roll, elbow, yaw, forearm roll, a direction check (`_wiggle_ask`), a power ladder (`_ladder`, `_score`) and a gravity fit (`fit_gravity`). Every number comes from the motor's own encoder and current (`CalInputs.q`, `CalInputs.tau`). [`tools/closed_loop_check.py`](../tools/closed_loop_check.py) judges the same `POS_MEASURED` signal.

**Measured now (all motor side):**
- Closed-loop ±10° steps on the left wrist: end errors 0.40–0.86°, always undershooting; 90% rise in 0.14–0.25 s.
- Power ladder: arc lag 0.032–0.054 rad, top sag 0.005–0.038 rad, friction 0.29–0.71 N·m, gravity-fit r² 0.77–0.91.
- During teleop, an idle arm sits 0.008–0.045 rad off its command; the moving arm lags about 0.1 s.

**Not measured at all:** the output link's angle, gearbox backlash, gearbox compliance, the absolute zero, kinematic errors, and where the claw really is. The log's `reached` field is forward kinematics of the motor encoders, so it cannot show any of these.

**Error budget at the claw** (lever 0.36–0.43 m from the shoulder to the claw tip, pending the measured TCP; **computed**):

**Run this test before building any camera feature: is the shoulder-roll zero 12–19° off?** Three findings point the same way (FK on the URDF copy, **computed** by this research):
1. URDF q = 0 is an A-pose: the upper arm points 15° out from vertical. The URDF roll limit (−15° left, +15° right) is exactly "upper arm vertical", and a freely hanging arm settles near roll ∓16°. Yet `_ArmFrames.capture_zero` ([`arm_driver.py:142`](../scripts/teleop/arm_driver.py)) sets `zero_fw = p_fw` with the arms hanging, and its docstring calls that pose URDF zero.
2. The saved gravity fits have a large constant bias on roll only: **−0.79 N·m (left) and +1.28 N·m (right)**, against 0.11–0.27 N·m on pitch and elbow ([`arm_power_profile.json`](../configs/arm_power_profile.json)).
3. A pure zero offset in the URDF model reproduces both biases: −0.79 N·m at −12°, and +1.28 N·m at about +19°. The right arm's fitted scale (1.25) also matches +19°. The left arm's scale (1.05) is lower than an offset alone predicts (1.18), so something else contributes there.

Cable drag, a current-sensor offset or friction asymmetry could also make a bias, so this is a hypothesis.

**If it holds, today's soft limits are wrong too.** The profile leaves `range_lower` and `range_upper` unset, so teleop uses the URDF roll limit (`TeleopIkSolver.set_range`, [`run_teleop.py:204`](../scripts/teleop/run_teleop.py)). Under today's hanging zero, that limit lets the upper arm swing ≈15° past vertical inward, toward the torso **(computed; it fits the torso hits; verify)**.

**Until the test says otherwise, tighten the inner roll limit by 15°.** Stop `run_teleop.py` under the §2.2 rule, set `range_lower` = 0.0 on `left_shoulder_roll` and `range_upper` = 0.0 on `right_shoulder_roll` in `configs/arm_power_profile.json`, then start it again. (Edit the file only while it is stopped: the running program saves the profile after every tuning change.) The clamp covers teleop and the power-ladder arc (`profile.ranges`, arm_calibration.py lines 124 and 434). It does not cover the direction check: `_wiggle_ask` clips to the URDF limits, and `_probe` ignores limits by design. So watch the roll joints during calibration until the zero is settled. Drop the clamp if the test rejects the hypothesis. If the test confirms it, keep the clamp until the corrected zero lands, and remove it in that same change: with the corrected zero, the URDF limit itself is "upper arm vertical".

**Test (5 minutes, motors off, no camera).** Stand the robot so the torso is vertical. Hang the arms as when taking zero. Read a phone inclinometer on the torso, then on each upper arm, in the frontal plane.

**Decision rule (preregister):**
- upper arm within ±3° of the torso's vertical → the hypothesis holds: zero is off by ≈15°;
- upper arm ≈15° outward (12–19°) → reject the hypothesis;

### 3.2 (a) Step 0: calibrate the stereo camera

**This comes first for everything vision-based.** Calibration v2, metric depth, point clouds, pointing models and the learned policies' camera inputs all depend on it.

**1. Identify the module (10 min).**
- `lsusb` shows `32e4:2b10`; at least one ELP stereo module enumerates as "3D USB Camera" under vendor `32e4` [9].
- The rig's 1280×480 and 2560×720 modes match ELP's OG02B10 global-shutter binocular module, which is listed at 3200×1200 MJPEG, 60 fps and **HFOV 120°** [10]. That match is *(unconfirmed)*.
- Run `v4l2-ctl -d /dev/videoN --list-formats-ext` and `v4l2-ctl -d /dev/videoN -l`. If 3200×1200 appears, assume global shutter and a wide lens. If not, assume a rolling shutter and use static frames only.
- Eye sync test: film a phone stopwatch, or wave a pen fast. Both halves of one frame must show the same instant.

**2. Lock the image.** Read the control names from `v4l2-ctl -d /dev/videoN -l`; on recent kernels they are usually `auto_exposure` and `exposure_time_absolute`. Example: `v4l2-ctl -d /dev/videoN -c auto_exposure=1 -c exposure_time_absolute=80` (1 = manual; the unit is 100 µs [11], so 80 = 8 ms). Then set `white_balance_automatic=0`, and turn autofocus off, if those controls are listed. Use at most 8 ms of exposure, and add light rather than exposure. M9/M12 lenses focus by hand: lock the ring with a drop of thread-locker after calibrating, because a turned lens voids the intrinsics. Store the full `-l` dump with the calibration.

**3. Target and method.**
- An A3 ChArUco board (for example 9 × 6 squares of 30 mm, 22 mm markers), printed flat and mounted rigidly. ChArUco corners are "much more accurate" than marker corners, and partial views near the image edge still count [12].
- Model: a pinhole with the rational model (k1–k6) if HFOV ≲ 100°, the fisheye model if ≈ 120° [13]. When unsure, fit both and keep the one with the lower held-out reprojection error.
- Procedure: each eye alone, then `stereoCalibrate` with `CALIB_FIX_INTRINSIC`, then `stereoRectify` and `initUndistortRectifyMap` [14]. Use 30–50 pairs at 0.3–0.8 m (the arm's working range), tilted up to ±45°, covering both eyes' corners.

**4. The upside-down mount.** [`depth_flow.grab`](../scripts/teleop/depth_flow.py) and `person_track` rotate the **whole** side-by-side frame by 180°, then split it. Calibrate on exactly those frames and never mix rotated and raw ones. Two sign checks:
- after `stereoCalibrate`, Tx must be ≈ −B (OpenCV convention);
- after rectification, a near object must show positive disparity.

If either fails, the halves are swapped.

**5. One calibration per video mode.** 640×480 per eye is 4:3 and 1280×720 per eye is 16:9, so the sensor crop differs. Calibrate every mode you use and store `configs/stereo_<W>x<H>.json` (new): K, distortion, R, T, the `stereoRectify` outputs, the V4L2 control values and the date.

**6. MEASURE the field of view and baseline.** They come out of the calibration: HFOV = 2·atan(w / 2f) from K, and the baseline B = |T|. Check B against a ruler on the lens spacing (±2 mm). Then replace the guesses: `person_track.py --hfov 78` (fitted from a servo sweep, not a lens calibration), the lab notes' 100°, and the "eyes are 6 cm apart" comment.

**7. Latency.** Frames reach other programs as `/dev/shm/bhl_camera.jpg`, whose capture time is its file mtime. Measure the lag once: rock a joint at 0.5 Hz and cross-correlate the camera-measured angle with the encoder angle. Use static or slow (≤ 0.05 rad/s) samples for backlash and compliance.

**Pass numbers (my thresholds; preregister them):** per-eye RMS ≤ 0.5 px; stereo RMS ≤ 0.6 px; median rectified row error ≤ 0.3 px; board size by triangulation within 2 / 4 / 8 mm at 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.8 m; X-corner static std ≤ 0.1 px over 100 frames; latency known to ±10 ms.

**Honest accuracy.** Stereo depth noise is σ_Z ≈ Z²·σ_d / (f·B), the same dependence RealSense documents [15]. It depends entirely on the measured f and B. With B = 60 mm (the guess):

| f (px) | What it means | Depth σ at 0.5 m, X-corner (0.15 px) | Depth σ at 0.5 m, marker corner (0.5 px) | Lateral, per 1 px at 0.5 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 185 | HFOV 120° at 640 px per eye (the rig's default 640×480 mode) | 3.4 mm | 11.3 mm | 2.7 mm |
| 395 | HFOV 78° at 640 px per eye (the rig's default 640×480 mode) | 1.6 mm | 5.3 mm | 1.3 mm |
| 790 | HFOV 78° at 1280 px per eye (only if such a mode covers the same field) | 0.8 mm | 2.6 mm | 0.6 mm |

Three cautions:
- The 2560×720 mode doubles f **only if** it covers the same field as 1280×480. A 16:9 mode may be a crop, so the gain is unknown until calibrated.
- Lateral precision is several times better than depth precision. For sub-degree backlash, measure the 2D image-plane motion of a high-contrast X-corner and average frames. Do not rely on stereo depth alone.
- In this research's synthetic test (rendered, JPEG-compressed images on a cluster CPU), X-corners localised to 0.06–0.13 px and ArUco marker corners to 0.4–0.7 px. Use ArUco only to find and identify targets; measure X-corners. A 2-minute static test on the real camera settles the real numbers.

Dense depth on cloth is a separate question, settled by step A7 (§3.9).

**Why fiducials and not a markerless network.** The best learned joint-angle estimator found, RoboPEPP (CVPR 2025), reports about 3.8° mean error, averaged over synthetic test sets, with no real-robot joint error reported [16]. That is worse than the ≈1.3° backlash you want to see. EasyHeC++ reports about 3 mm but its code is "coming soon" [17]. Kalib's code is released and reports about 3 mm and 0.5° in simulation, on a GPU [18]. So markerless methods are at best an **offline HPC cross-check** (Kalib) on recorded sessions. They also assume the URDF meshes match a 3D-printed robot whose claws are not in the meshes.

**First step:** A1 (the `v4l2-ctl` listing and the exposure lock), then print and back the A3 board. **Pass:** the pass numbers above. **Touches:** new `scripts/teleop/stereo_calib.py` (`capture()`, `solve()`), reading `camera_share.FRAME_FILE`; `person_track.py` `--hfov`. `opencv-python` 4.11.0.86 is already in [`uv.lock`](../uv.lock); check that `cv2.aruco.CharucoDetector` exists on the robot. **Measures now:** nothing; HFOV and baseline are assumed. **Would measure:** K, distortion, baseline, HFOV, reprojection error and latency, per mode.

- keep the claw in the central 60% of the image and the plate fully in view. If both cannot fit, add small tags on the fixed shoulder housings.

Tilt's extremes are recorded (`up_us` 1200, `down_us` 1850), so tilt is not clamped to `SAFE_TILT` = ±15° and reaches about 27° up and 31° down. Until pan is recorded, plan views inside ±35°. ### 3.4 (c) Output-side truth: what the camera sees against what the encoder says

**Targets (printed; filament plus a few prints, §1.4).**
- The reference plate (§3.3).
- A **claw cube** of 35–40 mm, clamped to the hand link, printed hollow or at ≤ 15% infill (roughly 10–20 g, **estimate**), or thin tag plates instead. A solid PLA cube of that size weighs about 55–80 g and adds about 0.2–0.3 N·m at the shoulder **(computed)**. Each visible face carries an ArUco tag (for identity) and a 2 × 2 checker (an X-corner, for measurement). Its offset in the hand frame is a calibration unknown.
  - Add its mass to the hand link in the gravity model while it is fitted. Either keep it fitted for teleop too, or refit gravity without it: B4 fits gravity with the cube on, so removing it afterwards makes `gravity_scale` wrong.
  - Check that the claw still closes fully with it mounted, and that it neither blocks the jaws nor hides the flag.
- X-corner stickers on the upper arm and the forearm.
- A forearm-roll **flag**: a 60–80 mm fin with 2–3 X-corners. The claw's centre of mass is only about 14 mm off the roll axis, so roll barely moves a point; it shows as rotation.
- Check that tags fit flat on the small printed parts, and that roll and claw motion do not hide them.

**One model explains every observation:** predicted pixel = π(K, T_cam←base(view) · FK(q_motor + δ − c(τ) − b(dir)) · p_target), for both eyes. Solve it with `scipy.optimize.least_squares` (TRF, `soft_l1` loss) on the N150; about 100 parameters and 1–2k residuals take seconds. This is the bundle adjustment. Identify, in stages so the problem stays well posed:

1. **Absolute zero offsets δ (5 per arm).** Static poses, each joint pre-loaded with about ±0.3 N·m so the gear sits on a known flank. Compare with today's zero: `ArmSupervisor.capture_zero` → `_ArmFrames.capture_zero` in [`arm_driver.py`](../scripts/teleop/arm_driver.py), which copies whatever the firmware reads into `zero_fw` with the arms hanging. Expected precision 0.1–0.3° **(computed)**, before the bias floor below.
2. **Backlash and hysteresis.** At a pose where |g| < 0.1 N·m, ramp the motor slowly (0.02–0.05 rad/s) through a reversal. The encoder moves; the output does not. Backlash = motor travel between the reversal and the onset of output motion; repeat 3 times for an interval. The existing windows help: `_ladder` runs out → top (1 s hold) → back → rest, and `_wiggle_ask`/`_probe` rock with a 2 s period. Limits:
   - raising kp to 60–80 (`JointPower.sanitized()` clamps kp at 80) makes the PD respond to small errors, but it does **not** raise the available torque;
   - the elbow (2.5 N·m) and forearm roll (1.25–1.5 N·m) saturate early, and 0.3 N·m of friction is a large share of the forearm-roll cap, so that test will be friction-limited. Check that the PD stays out of saturation.
   - Expect ≤ 1.3° if the arm gearboxes resemble the 6512 [1] (unconfirmed for the arms).
3. **Compliance.** A staircase of 5–6 static poses per loaded joint (pitch, roll, elbow) that spans the gravity torque. Fit θ_out − θ_motor = −τ/k + δ, with τ from `GravityModel` × `gravity_scale`; camera data alone gives backlash but not stiffness, which needs the torque. Varying τ separates k from δ. The ladder's 1 s top holds give free points.
4. **Kinematic errors.** Stage 1: offsets, TCP and target offsets, the plate pose and per-view camera poses (15–20 poses suffice). Stage 2: add compliance and backlash. Stage 3: full link deviations, only if held-out residuals stay above 3 mm. Precedents: a simulated iCub reached about 2 mm with 50 poses and 1 mm with 100 [19]; Agile Justin, a far more elastic humanoid, went from 21 mm to 3.1 mm mean error with a physical elastic model [20]. A stereo pan-tilt head watching wrist features is the classic DLR setup [21] *(details unconfirmed)*.

**Pose selection.** Sample about 500 reachable candidates inside a measured safe box in front of the robot, rank them by an observability index (O1) [22] *(unconfirmed)*, and keep 30–40 per arm plus 10 held out. Check the Jacobian's singular values and drop parameters below 1e-3 of the largest. Prefer views where the joint moves the target sideways in the image, and keep only poses A0 shows the camera can see.

**Collision check (the torso problem).** Every candidate pose, and the path to it, is checked before it is commanded:
- Pinocchio with hpp-fcl (2.4.4 in `uv.lock`) distance queries on the URDF collision geometry. The URDF copy has a torso box (0.15 × 0.14 × 0.23 m) and cylinders for upper arms and forearms, but **no collision shape for the hand/claw link**. Add a capsule for the claw and the claw cube.
- Add the environment, which the URDF does not have: the table plane at its measured height (§6.3 puts it 0.28–0.30 m below the shoulder), the stand or seat as a box, the head and pan/tilt camera as a box, cables where they hang, and the other arm near the midline.
- Require a clearance margin (for example 3 cm; preregister it) and plan paths as joint interpolations that keep it.
- In the first 0.1 rad of any long move, compare the camera-seen direction with FK (as in §3.5) and stop if they disagree.
- Build every new automatic stage on `PowerCalibration._move` and `_hold` ([`arm_calibration.py`](../scripts/teleop/arm_calibration.py), lines 658–689 and 691–713). They cap setup moves at `MOVE_SPEED` = 0.5 rad/s peak, and they freeze while the grip is released, the same pattern as `_wiggle_ask` (lines 738–742). `--max-speed` governs teleop only, not these moves. Dry-run every pose list in `--sim`, and keep a motor-power cutoff within reach (§2.2).
- Flux-offset sweeps (`tools/calibrate_arm_joint.py`) write motor flash, so they are outside this plan (§2.2). If you ever run one by hand, run this clearance check first: those sweeps cannot choose their direction, and they caused most of the torso hits on 22–23 Sep.
- Pink 3.4.0, the version in `uv.lock`, also has a `SelfCollisionBarrier` for the online IK [23]. It is a barrier (a control barrier function), not a task, and it needs the URDF collision model through hpp-fcl.

**Achievable angular accuracy** is σ_θ ≈ σ_p / r **(computed, at f = 395 px, 0.5 m)**: shoulder (r 0.36–0.43 m to the claw cube) 0.03° from lateral motion, 0.2–0.3° from depth; elbow (0.18 m) 0.06° / 0.5°; forearm-roll flag (0.07 m) 0.16° / 1.3°. The last two are FK of `q_cmd` and `q_meas`: encoder-based, not the true claw position. In the newest log the rows are jittery: median 49 ms, p90 88 ms.

**What it lacks:**
- the raw controller pose (SE3) and any rotation, including the IK target's rotation;
- the head pose, triggers, stick and claw pulse;
- the camera pan/tilt and any camera-seen claw position;
- a common clock. There are three: log rows use `perf_counter`, the header, person reports and `CameraAim.history` use `time.time()`, and a frame's capture time is the mtime of `/dev/shm/bhl_camera.jpg`. Headset packets carry no timestamp at all; `OperatorLink._receive` stamps arrival with `time.monotonic()`.

**Change.**
- Log every cycle while armed or calibrating, with a `held` flag (lift the `not any(self.solver.held)` guard).
- Add: the raw `self.hands[k]` (4×4), `self.head`, `self.triggers`, `self.grips`, `self.stick` (all from `parse_packet`/`parse_extras`); `solver.smooth`, `vr_ref`, `ee_ref`, `heading` and `targets[k].rotation`; the FK rotation (`hand_positions` returns translation only); the claw pulses (`ServoTeleop.update` / `_Sender.sent`); `CameraAim.angle`; the vision TCP and its age; the profile version; an intervention flag.
- Write `time.time()` and `time.monotonic()` at the same instant into the header, and use `time.monotonic()` everywhere else.
- In the page's `send()` and the bridge's `to_teleop_packet`, add `seq`, the XR frame time and the send time, so latency can be estimated.
- **Lag, for finer movement.** `SMOOTH_TAU` (run_teleop.py:215) adds a fixed ≈120 ms on top of a median 140 ms from target to reached. A One Euro filter could cut it, but sharper commands may raise MAXED saturation and shock on the 3D-printed gearboxes. Try it only after the timestamps are in; A/B it with `tools/teleop_tune.py audit` (MAXED share, p90 error); and keep the POST transport until the N150 CPU is profiled.
- Cost: about 80–90 MB per hour of JSON text **(estimate)**; convert to Parquet on the HPC.

**Registration of the headset frame to the robot frame.**
1. Clip a printed pointer to a controller. Pivot it about a fixed divot for 30 s; a least-squares fit gives the tip offset in the grip frame.
2. The robot holds 8 well-spread poses. Prefer **non-contact alignment**: hold the pointer tip 1–2 cm from the claw cube's top X-corner, and let the calibrated camera measure the gap. If you touch instead, do it only in a dedicated `REGISTER` state with these rules: the grips do not drive the arms; the robot moves to the next pose only after an A press, and only when headset tracking shows the touching controller more than 15 cm from the claw; moves run at ≤ 0.3 rad/s; and the waiting pose holds at the profile's floor torque. The arms are soft, so a firm touch moves them.
3. Solve the rigid transform with Kabsch/Umeyama (scale fixed at 1). This replaces the yaw-only `TeleopIkSolver.set_heading`, and puts the mirror (`FACING`) mapping in a measured frame.
- Expect a few mm to about 1 cm of residual. On a Quest 2 controller measured against motion capture, errors were 1.38 mm after a 5 mm move and 2.41 mm after 250 mm; the worst axis was 13.52 ± 6.57 mm after a 500 mm move about 700 mm from the headset; rotation 1.11 ± 0.37° [29].
- So the controller can register frames and fit mappings, but it cannot calibrate joints. A 0.05 rad zero error is 18–22 mm at 0.36–0.43 m, close to its noise: the camera stays the primary zero check, and the registration points become low-weight residuals in the bundle adjustment.

**A per-operator mapping beyond one `motion_scale`.** Today `ReachCheck._script` sets `motion_scale = clip(0.30 / reach, 0.25, 1.5)` at every arming (0.61 now), and `TeleopIkSolver.update` scales only translation; rotation is unscaled. One scalar cannot fit a workspace whose sideways and forward ranges differ, and shoulder pitch (−90°…+45°) and roll (−15°…+75°) are asymmetric.
- Fit per-axis scales, or an affine map x = A·u + b, on in-reach, slow samples (|q_cmd − q_meas| < 0.05 rad) where the operator showed intent by closing the claw. Store it per operator id in the profile, beside the reach that `ReachCheck` already measures.
- Fix windup at the same time. Over 13 logs (12,465 rows, about 11.6 min of driving), the IK target was more than 6 cm from what the IK could command in **25%** of samples, and operators re-grabbed about 20 times a minute. Shift `vr_ref` by the overshoot at the reach boundary, and add a haptic "wall" with `hapticActuators[0].pulse`, which works in Quest Browser (a wrong-hand bug was fixed on 2026-03-09 [30]).
- Validate on held-out sessions: the "sent out of reach" share and the target-to-camera-TCP error should both fall ≥ 30%.

**Mining the logs.** `tools/teleop_tune.py audit` already reports per joint `followed` %, the told/did correlation, time at the torque limit and p90 error, and per hand OUT OF REACH and trailing flags. Add the windup share, the target → reached lag by cross-correlation (median 140 ms in this research's pass over the logs), the loop-stall share (19% of intervals > 70 ms), grabs per minute, the per-axis gain from the affine fit, the camera-vs-`reached` error, and per-operator summaries.

**First step:** add `seq` and the timestamps (no behaviour change), and log one session. **Pass:** every row carries raw poses on one clock; the held-out "sent out of reach" share and the target-to-camera-TCP error each fall ≥ 30% after the mapping fit. **Touches:** `_open_log`, `_log_cycle`, `_log_row`, `TeleopIkSolver.set_heading`, `SMOOTH_TAU`, `ReachCheck._script`, `_watch_reach`; `quest_bridge.html` `send()`; `quest_bridge.py` `to_teleop_packet`; [`tools/teleop_tune.py`](../tools/teleop_tune.py) `audit`; new `tools/touch_register.py` and `tools/fit_mapping.py`. **Measures now:** scaled translations at 25 Hz while a grip is held. **Would measure:** raw 6-DoF hand and head poses, claw and camera state, one clock, the headset-to-robot transform, and a per-operator gain matrix.

### 3.8 An absolute zero from the encoder, modulo 24°

**The evidence.** The motor snapshots in the transcript show `position_measured = (n_rotations + raw/4096) · 2π / (−15)`. For example, raw 2505 with `n_rotations` 0 gives −0.25617 rad ([motorstuff 3402–3417](../2026-09-23-motorstuff-pastedcontent-idf751.txt)). The AS5600's raw count is a magnetic angle, absolute within one motor turn, and the gear ratio is an integer 15. So the output angle is known absolutely **modulo 2π/15 = 24°**, with 0.0059° resolution, whatever `n_rotations` is.

**Procedure.**
1. Measure each joint's true zero once, with the camera (§3.4). For a mechanical reference, move the joint to its stop **by hand**, with the motors in DAMPING or STOPPED. Never drive a joint into a stop under POSITION control: that can strip printed gear teeth. The stock BHL legs are zeroed against stops by `berkeley_humanoid_lite_lowlevel/scripts/calibrate_joints.py` (in the bhl-robustness-ladder copy). **Reference only: never run it, or anything that constructs `Humanoid()`, on this PC.** It does exactly that (`from berkeley_humanoid_lite_lowlevel.robot import Humanoid` at line 14, then `robot = Humanoid()` at line 17), and on this PC that maps can0/can1 to the legs.
2. Store `zero_ref_fw = p_fw(true zero) mod 24°` in the **host profile** (`JointPower`), never in motor firmware. This keeps the rule "never rewrite device_id or flux offset".
3. At every start-up, set `zero_fw = p_fw − wrap±12°(p_fw − zero_ref_fw)`, with each joint's window centred on its expected hanging angle (for left roll, possibly −15°, per §3.1).

**The wrap caveat.** A joint that starts more than 12° from its expected angle gets a zero that is wrong by exactly 24°, and nothing in today's code reliably catches it:
- `_arming_problem` ([`run_teleop.py:719`](../scripts/teleop/run_teleop.py)) refuses only a joint more than 0.1 rad outside its URDF range. A 24° wrap on shoulder pitch (−90°…+45°), elbow (0…90°) or roll (−15°…+75°) usually stays inside the range.
- The "THESE JOINTS ARE CARRYING LOAD" check runs only inside the power calibration (arm_calibration.py:265–270), not at ordinary arming, and it uses a +1.0 N·m margin.

So any joint can wrap silently, not only yaw and forearm roll. Therefore:
- keep today's relative zero as the default until the 5-power-cycle repeat test below **and** a camera check on all 10 joints both pass;
- after that, have the camera (or you) confirm all 10 joints at every boot;
- refuse to arm if any joint's modulo-24° zero differs from the hanging-pose zero taken the old way (with `q_hang`) by more than 6°. Hanging by eye is good to 1–3°, so a larger gap means a wrap or a bent arm.

Other dependencies:
- the magnet must not slip on the shaft: re-measure after any actuator rebuild;
- the gear ratio must be exactly 15;
- preload each joint before reading, because at the hanging pose gravity torque is about 0 and backlash leaves the gear flank undetermined (±0.65° if 6512-like).

**First step:** log `p_fw` at start-up over 5 power cycles with the arms hanging, and check that `p_fw mod 24°` repeats within backlash. Until that passes, the restart rule in §2.2 stands unchanged. **Pass:** per-joint std of the camera-measured offset ≤ 0.2° over 10 restarts, including ≥ 3 power cycles, with zero wraps. **Touches:** `_ArmFrames.capture_zero` (new `ref` argument), `ArmSupervisor.start`, `ArmSupervisor.capture_zero`, `_arming_problem`, `JointPower`. **Measures now:** a relative zero, as good as how still the arms hang. **Would measure:** a repeatable absolute zero.

### 4.4 Wire protocol and serving

**The stock servers are not safe as shipped:**
- LeRobot's async inference uses gRPC `add_insecure_port` and `pickle.loads` in both directions [40]. Anyone who reaches the socket can run code as you, and a fake server can run code on the robot PC.
- openpi's websocket server binds `0.0.0.0` with no authentication [41].
- GR00T N1.5's ZMQ server (the `n1.5-release` branch) binds `tcp://*:5555` with an optional plaintext token [42]. N1.7 also serves over ZMQ [43], but its bind and authentication defaults were not checked.

**Use a thin layer you own.** A new `serve_policy.py` (about 200 lines, on the HPC) loads LeRobot policies and serves them with `websockets.unix_serve` and a token check in `process_request` [44]. It speaks msgpack only, and checks every message for shape, finite values and size.
- **Request:** `{v, seq, t_obs, jpeg (bytes), state[12], task, executed_since_last, prev_left_over, d_est}`. The last two are RTC's inputs: the actions of the previous chunk not yet executed, and the expected delay in steps.
- **Response:** `{seq, t_obs, dt, actions[H×12], server_ms}`.
- **Send the camera's own JPEG bytes.** That costs the N150 no decode and is what you record, so training and serving preprocess identically on the GPU node. At an estimated 40–150 KB per 1280×480 frame and 2 requests/s, that is 0.6–2.4 Mbit/s of uplink. LeRobot's client pickles decoded arrays: 1.84 MB per 2×640×480 observation, 29 Mbit/s at the same rate.
- **Execute by time.** Action *i* belongs to `t_obs + i·dt`. Actions whose time has passed are skipped. The queue is "dry" when it holds no action stamped later than now, and then the arm holds (§4.5 item 5).
- **Rates.** Record and train at 30 Hz (the camera rate), use 50-action chunks (1.67 s), and request a new chunk every 10–20 executed steps. The ≈48 Hz loop interpolates between actions.

**Latency.** Home → Corvallis is **unmeasured**; this research estimates 120–300 ms, which is a delay of 4–9 policy steps at 30 Hz.
- Real-time chunking (RTC) was built for exactly this. In the real-robot tests, π0.5 at 50 Hz had a model latency of 76 ms (97 ms with RTC; the paper's latency table measures these on an RTX 4090), plus 10–20 ms of LAN. With 100 and 200 ms of injected delay (about 220 and 320 ms in total), RTC showed no degradation, while synchronous execution degraded linearly [45]. That was on a LAN, not home Wi-Fi.
- LeRobot's RTC supports π0, π0.5 and SmolVLA [46]. The networked version (PR #3112) was closed unmerged on 2026-09-26 [47], so today RTC runs in-process, and `serve_policy.py` applies it on the server side. ACT and X-VLA get no RTC in LeRobot: use plain time-indexed chunks for them.
- For comparison, Hugging Face reports a sub-100 ms round trip for async SmolVLA on a LAN with an RTX 4090 [48].

**Go/no-go for live serving (preregister).** From the robot PC, with the Quest streams running, send payloads of 2, 75, 150 and 300 KB at 2, 10 and 30 Hz to the echo server: 2 min per combination, then 10 min at the planned setting (about 150 KB at 2–3 Hz). Compare the p95 latency with the chunk's execution horizon. Live serving proceeds only if, at the planned setting, the p95 round trip plus the model's measured inference time stays under 10 executed steps (333 ms at 30 Hz), and a 10-minute `--sim` closed-loop dry run through the real tunnel has < 1% queue underruns. Otherwise use the §4.8 fallbacks.

**First step:** the echo server, speaking this protocol, in a 1 h CPU job, once COE has replied and §4.9 is closed. **Pass:** the go/no-go above. **Touches:** new `serve_policy.py` (HPC) and `policy_client.py` (robot), reading `camera_share.FRAME_FILE`. **Measures now:** nothing. **Would measure:** per-request latency from `t_obs` to actions on the robot, server decode and inference time, uplink bytes and queue underruns.

### 4.5 The safety ladder that stays on the robot

1. **A `POLICY` state** in `ArmSupervisor.tick`, beside `ARMED` and `CAL`, entered only from `ARMED` (by voice or a new button in `EVENTS`). A `PolicyLink` receives targets on a Unix datagram socket in a 0700 directory, or on UDP `127.0.0.1:11013` with a per-POLICY-session nonce read from a 0600 file, so no other local process (another session, a stray tool) can inject targets. It rejects any action that is non-finite or out of range, has `dt` outside [1/60, 1/15] s, a `seq` that does not increase, or a `t_obs` that matches no outstanding request. A separate `policy_client.py` holds the chunk queue.
2. **Dead-man and takeover.** A grip must be held for the policy to move the arm; releasing it means hold. Moving the controller beyond a threshold while holding hands control back to teleop, and those rows are logged with `intervention=True`. **That takeover is not bumpless in today's code.** `TeleopIkSolver.update` re-anchors `vr_ref` and `ee_ref` only when a grip goes from released to held (`if not self.held[k]`, [`run_teleop.py:235`](../scripts/teleop/run_teleop.py)). In POLICY the grip is already held, so the first teleop cycle would aim at the pose from before the policy moved the arm, limited only by the 0.35 rad lead and 1.5 rad/s. Fix: on entering POLICY and at the takeover, set `solver.held[k] = False` and `solver.smooth[k] = None` for both arms, so the next update re-anchors on the current pose.
3. **The same clamps as teleop.** Factor the last lines of `TeleopIkSolver.update` into a helper used by both paths: clip to the joint ranges, then to `max_speed·dt` (1.5 rad/s), then to `max_lead` (0.35 rad) around `q_meas`.
4. **The same torque path, plus a gentler POLICY limit.** `profile.feed_forward(gravity.torque(q_cmd))`, `profile.dynamic_limits` and `LimitWriter` keep the calibrated caps. In POLICY only, scale `headroom` (the part of the limit above gravity) by about 0.6, and `max_torque` too where the joint's gravity need allows, so the arm still holds itself up but pushes less hard on anything it meets. Also use a POLICY-only `max_lead` of about 0.15 rad.
5. **Staleness.** When the queue is dry (no action stamped later than now), HOLD: the arm keeps its current command with gravity feed-forward on. When no new chunk has arrived for 2 s, run a slow local retract to a preset pose above the table (≤ 0.3 rad/s, through the full clamp path, the pose collision-checked as in §3.4), then HOLD with gravity feed-forward until you end POLICY (triple X). Releasing the grip freezes the retract. DAMPING stays for CAN faults, `JointHealth` trips and `run_teleop` exit, because damping lets the arms sink onto the table and your hands. The headset link keeps its own 0.25 s / 5 s ladder, as today, and the firmware watchdog stays underneath. `JointHealth`, the CAN-miss stop and `_poll_state` still apply.
6. **Start gently.** Run the first closed-loop trials with `--max-speed 0.5` (the flag exists; its default of 1.5 rad/s is about 0.65 m/s at a 0.43 m claw), plus the POLICY torque scale and `max_lead` of item 4. Keep auditing logs with `tools/teleop_tune.py audit`. (`--cal-power-scale` does not help here: it scales only the power-calibration caps, run_teleop.py:1269.)
7. **A still, checked camera.** Force `CameraAim` to `still` at a fixed preset whenever recording or running a policy: a camera that tracks the operator changes the viewpoint between episodes. An SG90 does not return to the same angle reliably, so compare each session's first frame with a reference frame (the plate's pose) and refuse to start if it moved.
8. **Claws.** Clamp the policy's claw outputs to the open and closed pulses in `configs/claw_limits.json`, and rate-limit them.
9. **Never forward** :8080, UDP 11005–11014 or anything CAN-related through a tunnel. No `ssh -R` from the robot.

**Automated tests (`--sim`):**
- *Faults:* kill the client, kill the tunnel and `SIGSTOP` the server. Each must reach HOLD within one cycle of the queue running dry and start the slow retract at 2 s, with no target outside its range and no per-cycle step above `max_speed·dt`.
- *Takeover:* the policy drives an arm 0.3 rad away; then the controller moves past the threshold, 10 cm from where it was at the last anchor. Every cycle must satisfy |Δq_cmd| ≤ `max_speed·dt`, with no step toward the pre-policy pose.
- *Limits:* while `state == "POLICY"`, assert the torque scale, the `max_lead` and the claw clamps.
- *Injection:* a datagram from another process without the nonce (or from outside the 0700 directory) is rejected.

**First step:** the POLICY state in `--sim`, replaying a logged teleop episode as policy targets, with the four tests. **Pass:** all four pass. **Touches:** `ArmSupervisor.tick`, `_stop`, `OperatorLink`, `EVENTS`, `TeleopIkSolver.update` (the shared clamp helper, `held`, `smooth`), `profile.dynamic_limits`, `LimitWriter`, `ServoTeleop`, `CameraAim.set_mode`; new `PolicyLink` and `policy_client.py`. **Measures now:** nothing about policies. **Would measure:** latency per request, queue underruns, interventions, and the jump in `q` at chunk switches and takeovers.

### 4.7 The data → train → serve loop

1. **Record on the robot.** A new `recorder.py` copies the camera's JPEG bytes and a JSONL of state, actions, claw, mode and intervention flags, at 30 Hz on one clock. `_log_cycle` also sends a compact row to `127.0.0.1:11014` every cycle. 3. **Convert** in a CPU job on `share`: decode, rotate 180°, split the eyes, and write a LeRobotDataset v3.0 with two image keys, `observation.state[12]` and `action[12]`. Call `finalize()` [52].

5. **Serve.** The serve job writes its node and socket path to an endpoint file on the share, with mode 0600; the robot reads it and opens the tunnel. The server reads its token from a 0600 file in a 0700 directory, never from the command line, where other users of the node can see it with `ps`.

### 4.9 Close the LAN hole this week

Today any device on your Wi-Fi can reach three things:
- **UDP 11005.** `OperatorLink.__init__` binds `("0.0.0.0", 11005)` at [`run_teleop.py:316`](../scripts/teleop/run_teleop.py): arm commands.
- **The web app.** `quest_bridge.py` listens on `0.0.0.0:8080` (line 971), and also on `0.0.0.0:8443` when started with `--https` (line 976). Its routes (lines 952–966) accept poses on `POST /input` and `/ws`, and serve `/camera.mjpg` (the home camera), `/screen.mjpg` (the PC desktop), `/vision.jpg`, `/state.json`, `/sessions.json`, `/diag` and `/say.wav`, with no check at all.
- **The microphone path.** Binary frames on `/ws`, and `application/octet-stream` POSTs to `/input`, go through `handle_client_message` to `to_voice` (lines 316–330) and on to `voice_typer.py`. That program types text into the Claude Code pane with `tmux send-keys` (line 288), and sends "agent, …" commands such as power, gravity, sensitivity and undo to run_teleop's tuning port (lines 470–510). Audio frames cannot carry a token field, so a per-message token cannot protect them.

The fix, all in one change:
1. **Bind UDP 11005 to `127.0.0.1`.** The bridge already sends to `127.0.0.1` by default (`quest_bridge.py --udp-host`), and every other UDP socket in these scripts binds `127.0.0.1`.
2. **Authenticate once per connection, on every route.** The bridge makes a random token at each bring-up and shows it in the PC terminal (or on the face screen). You open `http://<robot>:8080/?k=<token>` once; `IndexHandler` checks it and sets an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie. A shared `prepare()` on every handler in the app table refuses any request without the cookie (or `k`). Tornado runs `prepare()` before each handler method, including the `/ws` upgrade, and `PoseSocket.open()` checks again. So an unauthenticated socket is closed before `handle_client_message` runs, and no audio reaches `to_voice`. Both listeners serve the same app, so the check covers `--https` too. A cookie set on :8080 is not sent to :8443, though, so the HTTPS page must itself be opened once with `?k=`. Log the source address of every refusal. Same-origin `fetch`, `EventSource`, `<img>` streams and the WebSocket handshake all send the cookie by themselves, so the page needs no change beyond being opened with `?k=`.
3. **Know its limit.** The cookie travels over plain HTTP. It stops casual devices on your network, not someone who can capture your Wi-Fi traffic.

**Tests:** extend [`tools/test_quest_page.py`](../tools/test_quest_page.py), which already runs the page in QuickJS, to open with the token. Add a Python test that refuses each of these: an untokened `GET /screen.mjpg` and `GET /camera.mjpg`, an untokened `POST /input`, an untokened WebSocket binary frame, and a UDP packet to 11005 on the LAN address. It also checks that `voice_typer` receives nothing on 11007.

**Order:** this is P0. P3b, which puts an OSU SSH key on the robot PC, waits until this is done on every route and both listeners.

**First step:** the 11005 bind and its `--sim` test (minutes), then the cookie check. **Pass:** all the tests above. **Touches:** `OperatorLink.__init__`; in `quest_bridge.py`, `main` (both `app.listen` calls), `IndexHandler`, a shared base handler with `prepare()`, `PoseSocket.open`, `parse_args`. **Measures now:** nothing; any LAN device is trusted. **Would measure:** refused requests, with their source addresses.

### 6.2 The gaps

- **Sim-to-real.** Even with a matched SO-101 in sim and in reality, the LeHome sim-round winner found the gap "still far too large for zero-shot transfer" and used real DAgger data [78].

### 6.3 Geometry: what the arms can reach (URDF FK; measure first)

- **Cloth zone:** about 20 cm deep × 30 cm wide, 0.08–0.28 m ahead of the shoulder axis, with the table 0.28–0.30 m below the shoulder pitch axis. On a 0.72–0.75 m desk that puts the shoulder axis at 1.0–1.05 m, which implies a stand (hence the mounting question).
- **Each claw owns its half.** With the URDF's 0.133 m tip, the tips reach at most about 2 cm past the midline; with a 0.10 m claw offset, the claws never get closer than 10–16 cm at table height. Either way: no handovers and no side-to-side folds. Front-to-back folds fit.
- **No wrist pitch:** the claw meets the table at a median 51–62° from vertical. A flush, top-down pinch is impossible over most of the zone.
- **Lift:** only 41–50% of the zone is reachable 10 cm above the table, so carry corners low (≤ 5 cm) and drag-and-lay.
- **No flinging:** FlingBot moves cloth 0.70 m at 1.4 m/s [79]; this claw tip tops out near 0.65 m/s at the 1.5 rad/s cap (0.70 m/s at full stretch).
- **Precision:** 1° of shoulder backlash moves the tip about 6–8 mm (0.36–0.43 m lever, up to 0.465 m at full stretch). A press-and-pinch therefore needs a **compliant mat 15–25 mm thick** and a current-based contact stop.

**A virtual floor first.** No table limit exists today. A target below the surface makes kp 30 and the 0.35 rad lead ask for up to about 10 N·m, which the dynamic limit then caps. So the arm presses into the table at full headroom for as long as the target stays there, loading the printed gearboxes and the thin jaw tips. Before Gate 0, add a table-plane clamp in `TeleopIkSolver`: TCP z ≥ z_mat − allowed_press, with allowed_press set from the measured p90 error and the mat thickness. A fixed 1 cm margin is not enough: the logged gap between commanded and reached positions (encoder FK) is a median 1.3 cm and p90 4.2 cm today **(measured, teleop logs)**. Add the contact stop of §6.4 item 5 at the same time.

**Gate 0:** measure the real claw-tip workspace before any cloth work, without touching. Hover the tip 5 mm above each mark of a 2 cm grid on the mat, by teleop, and measure the tip with the calibrated camera. That gives real reach and real positioning error, backlash and zero included.

**First step:** the table-plane clamp, tested in `--sim`. **Pass:** Gate 0's claw-tip p90 error is smaller than the press depth the mat allows. **Touches:** `TeleopIkSolver.update` (the clamp, with z_mat stored in the profile); `ArmSupervisor.tick` (the contact stop). **Measures now:** nothing. **Would measure:** reach and positioning error over the grid.

### 6.4 Modifications to the claws, mat, table and camera (cheapest first)

1. The claw TCP frame (§3.6).
2. **Nail jaw tips:** 1–1.5 mm thick, 10–15 mm long, chamfered, one jaw longer so it touches first. SpeedFolding extended its fingertips with "small 3D printed teeth" [80].
3. Silicone or rubber friction pads on the inner jaws.
4. **A compliant, matte mat** 15–25 mm thick, in a colour that contrasts with the cloth. SpeedFolding used a sponge mattress so the grippers could reach below the cloth [80]; FlingBot used a rubber mat [79].
5. **A contact stop, required before any pressing:** watch |τ − τ_ff| on the elbow and shoulder while pressing. On a rise, hold and retract 1 cm. Never stay in contact for more than 2 s.
6. **Fiducials** at the four mat corners for an image-to-table homography, re-estimated every frame, because the servo head moves.
7. **A fixed view of the zone.** The tilt's down mark is about 31° below level (at the assumed 0.09°/µs), which may not see the zone (A0, §3.3). Re-bracket the tilt 30–45° downward, or add a fixed overhead USB camera.
8. **A grasp check** by camera (did the corner lift?). Optionally a feedback servo for the claw, such as the STS3215 ($13.89 [2]); it needs a bus adapter and a new mount, and its readback is *(unconfirmed)*.
9. Wrist cameras later, with their mass budgeted against the forearm cap.

### 6.5 A staged plan, from washcloth to garments

This is the one stage ladder for the whole plan; §5.3 gives each stage's model, compute and GPU-hours. Every stage has one primary endpoint, preregistered with a fixed n; the other numbers are secondary.

| Stage | What | Demos | Primary endpoint | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Cloth-ready rig (§6.4 items 1–7), the virtual floor and contact stop (§6.3), Gate 0's reach grid, the recorder; then 20 scripted corner grasps | — | corner-grasp rate out of 20, called p0 below (a measurement) | claw-tip p90 error smaller than the press depth the mat allows |
| 1a | Scripted front-to-back half fold of one washcloth, using SpeedFolding's tilted slide-and-pinch | — | full folds out of 20 interleaved trials (secondary: corner-grasp rate) | none: it is the baseline the later stages must beat |
| 1b | Learned pinch-and-lift of a washcloth corner: ACT against SmolVLA | ≥ 50 | lifts of ≥ 5 cm held for 2 s, out of 20 per policy | the better policy reaches a rate preregistered from p0 before the trials (for example ≥ p0); SmolVLA against ACT by a one-sided Fisher test at fixed n = 20 |
| 2 | Learned front-to-back half fold: the better 1b policy against X-VLA | 100–150 | full folds out of 20 | beats the Stage-1a script (one-sided Fisher test, fixed n = 20) |

| 4 | Improvement loop | rounds of 20–30 | interventions per episode | the intervention rate falls each round |

**Garments** are a decision, not a stage: they are not realistic on these arms. A pair of SO-101 arms beside the robot ($121.94 in parts per follower arm [2]) would reuse the LeHome pipeline end to end. LeHome's real finalists scored 470–895 of 1080 points (44–83%) [82][72].

**Replaying recorded motion** (Stage 0's check that a recorded `q_cmd` reproduces the motion): replay in `--sim` first. On hardware, replay only within the same session, after the zero check, at `--max-speed 0.5`, through the POLICY clamp path, with the grip dead-man held.

**Priors.** Corner grasp 10–40% **(estimate)**. No full-fold prior is given, and every threshold is set from this rig's own Stage-0 and Stage-1a numbers, not from the anchors below. Those anchors all used arms with wrist pitch and parallel-jaw grippers, most of them with wrist cameras; π\*0.6's rig, for example, has two 6-DoF arms with parallel-jaw grippers and two wrist cameras [57]. This rig has a 51–62° approach angle, no wrist pitch, open-loop claws with no grasp sensing, 6–8 mm of tip error per degree of backlash, and one head camera that may not see the zone.

**Anchors:**
- SpeedFolding (ABB YuMi with parallel-jaw grippers): 93% on a T-shirt and 87% on an unseen towel [80].
- ICRA 2024 cloth competition: the winner reached 0.69 grasp success; hand-engineered methods took 1st and 3rd place, a learned one 2nd [83]. That is one reason Stage 1a is a script.
- Two SO-101 arms + SmolVLA with 50 demos: 2.27/3 mean reward over 7 episodes, against 0.63/3 for ACT over 10 (a blog; tiny n) [84]. Two Piper arms + ACT with 120 demos: 10/10 in one continuous take (self-report) [85].
- Diffusion Policy folding a T-shirt from 284 demos teleoperated with Quest Pro controllers: 75% over 20 trials [86].

**Protocol:** the same cloth; a printed placement template; policies interleaved; every failure kept; scored blind from before/after top-down photos, with LeHome-style keypoint-pair conditions adapted to a square cloth.

**First step:** Stage 0's reach grid, hovering. **Pass:** each stage's gate above, preregistered. **Touches:** `TeleopIkSolver` (TCP frame, virtual floor); a new scripted-primitive module (for example `scripts/teleop/cloth_primitives.py`) using `vision_targets.py` for the mat homography; `ServoTeleop` / `ServoLink.move` for the claw; a contact stop on `driver.tau` in `ArmSupervisor.tick`. **Measures now:** nothing about cloth. **Would measure:** the claw-tip reach-grid error, per-corner grasp success and full-fold success.

### 6.6 Where the field is, and your Isaac Sim pipeline

The prize-winning 2026 recipe (1st in sim, 2nd in the real final) is **π0.5 + RL from rollouts (AWR + RECAP) + DAgger corrections**. It reached 79.63% in sim (1st of 62) and 865/1080 in the real final, trained on one H200 with about 12,500 rollout episodes [78][72]. FoldNet++ adds 120K synthetic T-shirt episodes over 6 embodiments and reports over 90% real success [87]. This rig can copy the **loop** (demos → policy → interventions → retrain), not the scale, the wrist cameras or the arms. Sim co-training stays a labelled add-on experiment: Maddukuri et al. report +38% on average from sim data, on tasks that include no cloth [88].

**Using your Isaac Sim pipeline.** The winner's `lehome_sim` checkpoint (Apache-2.0; a JAX checkpoint that probably needs openpi rather than LeRobot) [89] and a separate `lehome_real` checkpoint [72] are public. They expect three 224×224 cameras, including wrist cameras, on SO-101 arms [78], so they cannot run on this rig.
- An optional, labelled experiment, not on the critical path: reproduce the 79.63% with your preregistered LeHome evaluator on dgxh, with a blind scorer, as a check of your pipeline. Your cluster renders with Storm, and the renderer gap alone collapsed action skill before, so a miss may be the renderer rather than the checkpoint.
- A BHL twin in your scene (5-DoF, no wrist pitch, 0.30 m reach, one head camera) needs full retraining. It is worth it only after Gate 0 (§6.3), and only if your renderer-gap meter shows that sim frames are close enough to real ones.

## 7. The "point model": three candidates

Your request said "also saw something about a point model". It looks typed (the misspellings read as typing slips, not dictation errors), so "point" may be literal rather than "pi zero point five" heard aloud. Most likely first:

**(a) π0.5, "pi zero point five".** Physical Intelligence's open vision-language-action model (openpi, weights Sept 2025) [49]. Your LeHome documents name it as the winning paper's base model and record it as blocked: lerobot 0.4.3 looked for a patched transformers fork. That blocker is gone upstream: LeRobot PR #2964 (merged 2026-03-05) moved every policy to stock Transformers v5, and the current `pi` extra needs no fork, but needs Python ≥ 3.12 [90][91]. Checkpoints trained on the old stack will likely need fine-tuning again. *Helps this rig?* Yes, as the strongest open policy to fine-tune on the HPC (§6.5, Stage 3). Never on the robot PC.

**(b) A point-cloud model.** PointWorld (NVlabs, January 2026) predicts how every 3D point of an RGB-D scene will move under a planned robot action; the code is Apache-2.0 with released checkpoints, and it was shown on a Franka arm [92]. iDP3 is the closest relative of this rig: a humanoid teleoperated in VR, with egocentric 4096-point clouds from a head-mounted L515 depth camera, running at 15 Hz on board. Its authors call RealSense D435 depth "very imprecise" [60]. *Helps this rig?* Not yet: both need good metric depth, and a 6 cm USB stereo pair on textureless cloth will likely fall short. The dense-depth check (A7, §3.9) settles that. If it fails, the cheapest fix is an on-device depth camera: Orbbec Gemini 335 ($264, 0.10 m minimum range) [3], OAK-D Lite ($269; about 20 cm minimum, and only in extended-disparity mode) [4], or RealSense D405 ($325, 7–50 cm) [5].

**(c) A pointing or keypoint model.**
- MolmoAct2 (Ai2, May 2026) is an open "action reasoning" VLA; its first version reasoned through depth and 2D trajectory traces before acting. It is Apache-2.0, a LeRobot policy with an SO-100/101 checkpoint, and needs a GPU [66].
- Gemini Robotics-ER 2 (public preview, 2026-07-30) is a hosted API that returns points as [y, x] on a 0–1000 grid [93]. It sends home camera frames to Google, and its latency is likely seconds (estimate).
- Open pointing models that can run on the HPC: Molmo2-8B [94], MolmoPoint [95] and RoboPoint [96] *(unconfirmed)*.
- Point Policy learns keypoint policies from 15–30 human videos per task, in a calibrated two-camera scene [97].

*Helps this rig?* Pointing can supply the "where" for a cloth task (towel-corner pixel → stereo 3D point → IK target) once Step 0 is done. The missing wrist pitch still limits how the claw can grasp what is pointed at, so each point also needs a reachability check before it becomes a target.

**A fourth, smaller meaning: point trackers.** Models such as DeepMind's TAPIR and TAPNext (Apache-2.0 [98]) follow chosen pixels through a video. Kalib uses one for markerless camera calibration [18], so trackers help calibration v2 as an offline HPC cross-check.

**One cheap first test, whichever you meant.** Save 20–50 frames of a towel on the table (copy them from `camera_share.FRAME_FILE`, which is overwritten every frame) and label the corner pixels by hand. Then measure the pixel error and the p50/p95 latency of Gemini Robotics-ER 2 and of one open model on an A40. Keep anything private out of the frames that go to Google.

**First step:** that labelling pilot. **Pass:** median corner error < 15 px at 640×480, with p50/p95 latency reported. **Touches:** saved frames from `camera_share.FRAME_FILE`; a new `tools/point_eval.py`; later, a voice intent ("agent, grab the towel corner") that maps a pixel → a stereo 3D point (§3.2) → a `TeleopIkSolver` target that you approve with the grip. **Measures now:** nothing. **Would measure:** pointing error in pixels (in mm after Step 0) and latency.

Which one you meant is item 10 in §1.3.
